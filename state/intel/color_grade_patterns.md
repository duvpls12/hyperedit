## Grade Hierarchy

```
1. neutral          ≈ 50%  — baseline clean, no stylization
2. natural          ≈ 22%  — slightly warm, organic
3. vivid            ≈  6%  — boosted saturation, punchy exteriors
4. warm             ≈  5%  — golden hour, interiors
5. saturated        ≈  4%  — rich pools, lush landscaping
6. dark             ≈  2%  — moody/evening shots
7. warm_neutral     ≈  1%  — compromise grade
8. black and white  ≈  1%  — title cards, architectural details
```

## Key Insights

- **72% of frames are neutral or natural** — the visual language of this market is clean, not stylized.
- **Vivid/saturated reserved for exterior and pool shots** — where color punch sells the lifestyle.
- **Warm grades for interiors** — creates inviting, lived-in feel.
- **Dark/moody appears in <2%** — twilight or architectural drama shots.
- **B&W used only on titles / cards** — never on property footage.

## Grade Recommendations

| Context | Recommended Grade |
|---|---|
| Aerial / exterior wide | vivid or saturated |
| Pool / water features | saturated |
| Interior living room | warm or warm_neutral |
| Kitchen / bathroom | neutral |
| Bedroom | natural or warm |
| Night / twilight | dark |
| Title cards | black and white or neutral |
| Overall master grade | neutral + scene-specific lifts |

## LUT Strategy

Based on the corpus, the optimal LUT strategy is:
1. **Base LUT**: neutral (clean, no heavy color cast)
2. **Exterior override**: +10-15% saturation on sky/grass
3. **Interior warmth**: +5-8% warm tint on living spaces
4. **Pool/water**: teal-boost secondary grade
5. **No heavy film-emulation LUTs** — corpus shows the market prefers clean digital over filmic look
